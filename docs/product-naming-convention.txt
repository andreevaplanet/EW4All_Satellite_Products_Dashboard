EW4All Satellite Products Dashboard — product naming convention
Internal reference. Applied 26 August 2026 across all 193 product cards.


1. THE CONVENTION

  Case            Title Case for the product phrase, with articles, prepositions and
                  conjunctions in lower case (of, at, in, for, and, by, from, the, to).
                  About three quarters of the catalogue already read this way; the rest
                  were brought into line.

  Word order      Instrument or satellite first where the provider names one, then the
                  variable, then qualifiers in parentheses. This was already the CMA and
                  EUMETSAT pattern; it is now also applied to JAXA and KMA products where
                  it was needed to tell two identically-named products apart.

  Processing      "Level 1", "Level 2", "Level 3" always spelled out, never L1/L2/L3, and
  level           placed after the instrument and before the variable.

  Acronyms in     Retained only where the acronym is a provider product code (KMA's (RR),
  brackets        (CTH), (TPW); EUMETSAT's H SAF codes (H60B), (H34)) or is reused later
                  in the same name. Removed where it merely repeats words spelled out
                  immediately before: "Sea Surface Temperature (SST)" became "Sea Surface
                  Temperature", which now matches "Land Surface Temperature".

  Units           Space between number and unit: 25 km, 3.9 µm, 5000 m, 24 h. Lower-case
                  unit symbols. Superscripts for powers: m³/s, km². Units in parentheses,
                  not square brackets. Units removed from a name where they described the
                  variable rather than identified the product (NASA's "(K)").

  Wavelengths     "µm", never "um" and never the CJK compatibility character "㎛".

  Number          Singular for a single variable ("Cloud Top Temperature"), plural only
                  where the product genuinely delivers a set ("Aerosol Properties",
                  "Vegetation Health Products").

  Compounds       Closed where standard usage closes them: shortwave, longwave, wildfire,
                  nowcasting. Hyphenated modifiers in Title Case: "Real-Time",
                  "AMV-Based", "All-Weather", "10-Day".

  Punctuation     "and" rather than "&". Commas rather than slashes in lists of variables.
                  No space around a solidus joining two values (12.5/25 km). Space before
                  an opening parenthesis. En dash before a trailing regional qualifier.

  Regional        IODC carried as a hyphenated prefix ("MSG-IODC ..."), not as a trailing
  qualifiers      suffix, since the majority of those products already read that way.


2. WHAT WAS NOT CHANGED

Official provider product names were left alone even where they break the pattern: the
MODIS collection names (MOD14A1, MYD14A1, MCD14ML, MCD64A1), FIRMS, GSMaP, GeoNEX,
NEX-GDDP-CMIP6, ERSST, ICOADS, NOAAGlobalTemp, the NOAA tropical cyclone techniques (ADT,
MTCSWA, eTRaP, HISA, TCFP), CLARA-A3, SARAH-2 and SARAH-3. Long channel enumerations were
left as enumerations; they are already recorded as a source-structure issue rather than
treated as names.

126 of 193 names changed. 67 were already consistent with the convention.


3. FLAGGED, NOT CHANGED

  CMA "Normalized Vegetation Index" (4 products). The variable is almost certainly the
  Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), the standard term. Not renamed, because
  the wording may be CMA's own product name. The US "-ized" spelling is also left as CMA
  wrote it, unlike "Sulphur" and "Vapour" elsewhere, because the whole name is under query
  and changing its spelling while querying its wording would be confusing. Confirm both
  with CMA.

  KMA "AMV WV069". The code implies the 6.9 µm water vapour channel, but unlike the other
  three AMV products the wavelength is not written out in the source, so it is not
  asserted in the name. Confirm with KMA.

  "Sulfur" versus "sulphur". KMA and NASA write "Sulfur", EUMETSAT writes "Sulphur". The
  KMA product name was set to the WMO house spelling ("Sulphur Dioxide Detection"); the
  NASA name was left as the provider wrote it because it forms part of a named service
  ("AIRS Rapid Response"). Confirm whether provider spelling or house style should win.

  ISRO "Fire". A one-word name that says nothing about what the product delivers —
  detection, radiative power or burned area. Not expanded, because expanding it would mean
  inventing content. Ask ISRO for the product's proper name.

  Two names are still shared by two products each: "Land Surface Temperature" (NASA and
  IMD/ISRO) and "Sea Surface Temperature" (NASA and IMD). Both are generic variable names
  used by different providers, and the provider is shown on every card, so they are left
  as they are rather than padded with instrument prefixes the sources do not give.

  Roshydromet's four-products-in-one-cell and NOAA's three-in-one entries were Title Cased
  and their stray backtick removed, but they were not split; they remain on the validation
  sheet as source-structure issues.

  EUMETSAT p144, "Surface Radiation Dataset - Heliosat (SARAH-3 CDR)", keeps its
  hyphen-space because "Surface Radiation Data Set - Heliosat" is the CM SAF product's own
  name and the dash is part of it. It is the one deliberate exception to the punctuation
  rule.


5. TERMS USED ONE WAY ONLY

Applying the rules name by name left five cases where the same concept was still written
two ways across comparable products. Each is now settled on one form:

  Near real time    Spelled out as "Near Real-Time" in every name; NRT retained only
                    inside a provider product code, "(NRT S3 FRP)". Three EUMETSAT
                    aerosol and trace-gas products changed.

  Nowcasting SAF    "NWC SAF" throughout, the form the SAF uses itself. The cloud
                    products entry previously read "Nowcasting SAF".

  Global            Spelled out throughout. The Cloud Analysis Product entry previously
  Instability Index abbreviated it to GII while two sibling products spelled it out.

  High resolution   Hyphenated where it modifies a following noun — "High-Resolution
                    Cloud Analysis Information", "ASCAT Ultra High-Resolution Winds",
                    "SAR High-Resolution Tropical Cyclone Wind Speeds". Left unhyphenated
                    in "Altimetry Level 2 High Resolution", where it is a trailing noun
                    phrase naming EUMETSAT's own product variant rather than a modifier.
                    This is the second deliberate exception, alongside p144.

  Multi-product     Semicolons separate the products named in a single source cell. Applied
  cells             to the Roshydromet tropical cyclone entry and to NOAA's "MiRS
                    Precipitation Rate; GAASP Precipitation Rate; Snowfall Rate", which
                    previously ran together as one unpunctuated string. Neither is split
                    into separate records; both remain on the validation sheet.

An audit script now checks every rule in section 1 and these five term pairs against all
193 names, so the next person changing a name can re-run it rather than reading the
catalogue by eye.


4. FULL LOG

Every change is listed in ew4all-name-changes.csv, one row per product:
ID, provider, original name, revised name, reason.

Changes by provider: EUMETSAT 44, KMA 17, JAXA 17, CMA 16, NOAA 13, NASA 10, JMA 4, Roshydromet 3, IMD 2.

A second pass caught eight products the first pass missed — MWRI Cloud Liquid Water still
reading "Ascend", MERSI-II "Water Vapor" against the house spelling used elsewhere, four
lower-case words mid-name with Title-Cased comparators in the catalogue, and the two
Global Instability Index products still carrying hyphen-space suffixes. The audit is now
run mechanically against each rule in section 1, and the only remaining flag is the
deliberate p144 exception above.
